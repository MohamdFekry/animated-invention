Lesson 6

MLM keeps tiny fleet results humble.

A fleet with four buckets can look extreme by chance. A multilevel model partially pulls small-sample estimates toward the overall pattern.

The simple idea

Big samples speak for themselves. Small samples borrow strength.

This is called partial pooling. It is powerful, but it is also harder to explain, so it should be a later E2 feature.

Plain English

Do not overreact to tiny slices.

Fleet C might show -8 minutes with only four buckets. That does not mean we should fully trust -8. MLM asks how much evidence the fleet has before trusting its extreme result.

observed pooled toward overall
small sample = more shrinkage toward overall effect

Try this

Set buckets to 2 and effect to +10.

The observed result is extreme, but the pooled estimate moves back toward the overall effect because the sample is tiny.

When to use it

Use it when many fleets are small.

If each fleet has enough data, fleet-level analysis plus CIs may be enough for the first version.

Product lesson

Keep MLM as an advanced option.

It is statistically useful, but harder to explain. Add it after the simpler E2 workflow is trusted.
