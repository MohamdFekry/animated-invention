Lesson 4

A result needs a range, not just one number.

A point estimate is our best guess. A confidence interval tells us how much room there is for the true effect to be different.

Simple reading rule

If the range crosses zero, be careful.

Crossing zero means the data still allows “no real effect” or an effect in the opposite direction.

Plain English

The dot is the estimate. The line is uncertainty.

More buckets and less noise make the line shorter. Fewer buckets and more noise make the line longer.

Short line: easier to trust.

Wide line: still uncertain.

more buckets + less noise = tighter CI

CI width simulator

A simple bucket-level approximation for intuition.

-40+4
Estimate +1.2
95% CI +0.4 to +2.0
Status Conclusive
The interval is entirely above zero, so this looks conclusive.

Try this

Lower bucket count to 12.

The estimate stays the same, but the interval gets wider. The conclusion becomes less confident.

Switchback care

Use bucket-level uncertainty.

Do not calculate a naive task-level CI that pretends every task is a fully independent experiment.

Product lesson

Show estimate plus CI for every metric.

A metric card without uncertainty can make a weak result look too certain.
